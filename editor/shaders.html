Shaders: the language on the GPU

Each picture below is a program in the language. It says what colour the point (x, y) is at a given time, either as one definition colour that gives a Colour record, or as three definitions red, green and blue. The compiler turns that into WGSL, WebGPU's shading language, and the GPU runs it once for every pixel, every frame.

Only part of the language can go to a GPU: Float64s and Bools, records of them, arithmetic, if, lets, and calls. There is no recursion, because a GPU has no call stack. A function that calls itself as its last step is fine, though, because that is a loop, and it becomes one. Anything else is refused, and the compiler says why, the way it reports a type error.

A ray marcher, with normals from the derivative

Starting…

For each pixel, march follows a ray from the camera. scene says how far the nearest surface is, so the ray can safely step that far, and then it asks again. Where a surface faces is the gradient of scene. Most ray marchers estimate it by sampling scene at six nearby points. Here it is exact: shade asks for unit(gradient((q) ↦ scene(q, time))(p)), the slope of scene along each axis as one Vec, and the compiler generates the gradient's code from scene before printing the shader, so it runs on the GPU like everything else and follows any edit to scene. (Differentiating a program before compiling it for a GPU is what Enzyme does.) march and shadow call themselves as their last step, so on the GPU they are loops.

The Mandelbrot set, on complex numbers







Starting…




Each pixel is a complex number c. escape repeats z ↦ z · z + c from 0 and counts the steps until z is more than 2 from the origin; the points that never get there are the Mandelbrot set, drawn black. The picture zooms into Seahorse Valley and back out every 30 seconds. The · and + are the same ones every number type has. Here they are at Complex, so they are the complex product and sum, and on the GPU a Complex is a vec2f. The colours are Ersagun Kuruca's HSI colour space: a circle of hues at one luminance, so every colour is equally bright.

The WGSL the GPU runs

On the left is the WGSL the compiler printed. A Float64 is f32, a record is a struct, a let is a let, and an if is an if statement. Calls stay calls. On the right is the part the page adds around it: one triangle that covers the screen, and a fragment shader that turns each pixel's position into x and y and calls the program.






Credits: sphere tracing is John C. Hart's ("Sphere tracing", The Visual Computer, 1996). The ring's distance, the smooth blend and the soft shadow are Inigo Quilez's formulas, from his distance functions, smooth minimum and soft shadows articles; each definition that uses one says so. The Mandelbrot set is Benoit Mandelbrot's (1980), drawn by counting escape steps, the standard method, and its colours are Ersagun Kuruca's HSI colour space (from his hsi-playground), with Rec. 709's luminance weights and the sRGB transfer function. The scene, the ripples and the code are written for this project. The WGSL follows the W3C spec. Everything else the language borrows is listed in CREDITS.md.
