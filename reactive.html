Cells: reactivity and values on demand

Both pages below are programs in the language, run in your browser by its evaluator. They use one effect, Cells: cell(x) makes a cell you can write, formula(() => …) makes one computed from others, read(c) reads either, and write(c, x) changes a cell (that one is the effect Update).

Is this Solid.js? It's the same model. In Solid, reading a signal inside an effect or a memo records the dependency, through a global "current listener". Here read is an operation of an effect, and the runtime's handler of that effect records who read what. A handler's dynamic scope is exactly that "current listener". A cell is Solid's signal, a formula is its memo, and each changing part of the page is one of its effects. What the language adds is that the type says so: a view is () -[Cells]-> Text, it can only read; a button is () -[Cells, Update]-> Unit; a formula can't write, and nothing may write while a search (Choice) is still open.

And values on demand? The same handler gives them. A formula runs when it is first read, and its value is kept until a cell it read changes, which is what "lazy" means in Haskell. A continued fraction whose rest is a formula is an infinite number whose terms are worked out when you ask for them, and only once.

Parts of a page that run again flash. The line under each page counts what ran.

A counter, a detail that can hide, a todo list

Click +: the count, “doubled” and “count squared” run again, nothing else. Hide the details and click + again: “count squared” no longer runs, because while hidden it doesn't read count. Dependencies are found while running, not declared.










Continued fractions, computed on demand






√n = [a₀; a₁, a₂, …] means a₀ + 1/(a₁ + 1/(a₂ + …)). The sums and products are exact, by Gosper's algorithm (HAKMEM, 1972): each term of the result is taken from just enough terms of the inputs. Move terms up by one: only the new terms are computed. At n = 4, √4 × √4 gives [4]; at any n that isn't a square, no first term ever comes, because the terms of √n · √n agree with n forever, and equality of exact reals can't be decided. with fuel gives up after 300 steps.
