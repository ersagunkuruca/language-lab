Shaders: the language on the GPU

The picture below is a program in the language. Its three definitions red, green and blue say how much of each colour the point (x, y) has at a given time. The compiler turns them into WGSL, WebGPU's shading language, and the GPU runs them once for every pixel, every frame.

Only part of the language can go to a GPU. That part is Numbers and Bools, arithmetic, if, lets, and calls. There is no recursion, because a GPU has no call stack. A function that calls itself as its last step is fine, though, because that is a loop, and it becomes one. Anything else is refused, and the compiler says why, the way it reports a type error.

What the GPU runs








On the left is the WGSL the compiler printed. A Number is f32 and a let is a let. An if is an if statement. Calls stay calls. Below is the part the page adds around it: one triangle that covers the screen, and a fragment shader that turns each pixel's position into x and y and calls the three functions.










Everything on this page is written for this project. The full-screen triangle is the usual graphics trick of one oversized triangle instead of two that make a square. The WGSL follows the W3C spec.
